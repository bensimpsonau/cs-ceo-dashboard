Context

Boys — here's the refreshed framework. Built honestly on the actual per-sale economics: every dollar of new-sale revenue pays GST, merchant fees, closer/setter commission, and the ad spend that generated it. Alchemy's commission is paid on what's left after all of that — the real contribution margin — not the headline top-line number.

Three moving parts:

  1. Full unit economics before commission. We strip GST, merchant fees, sales team commissions, ad spend, and a 15% Platform Fee Allocation off the gross first. Alchemy is then paid a tier rate on the remaining contribution margin.
  2. Tiered commission (cliff at A$500K/mo gross). 12% of contribution under A$500K gross new sales, 15% of contribution at or above. The whole amount flips at the threshold — real reward for hitting scale.
  3. MER Floor Protection. If monthly MER drops below 2.0x, no commission is paid. Alchemy gets a flat A$10,000 retainer instead — protects both sides from subsidising bad ad months.

All numbers lead with USD (the US market is our growth engine). AUD shown as secondary. FX: 1 USD = 1.587 AUD. Planning base: 3x MER.

MER Floor Protection

Why the 2.0x floor exists

MER = Revenue ÷ Ad Spend. Below 2.0x, each $1 of ad spend returns less than $2 of revenue. After paying GST, merchant, sales commissions, and platform costs, the unit economics go negative. At that point, paying commission on contribution would be paying Alchemy to scale a losing media buy.

Rule: If monthly MER < 2.0x, no commission is calculated. Alchemy receives A$10,000 flat for that month. Each month stands alone — no carry-forward debt, no penalty. When MER recovers, commission structure resumes.
